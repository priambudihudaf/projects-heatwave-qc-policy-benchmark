# Targeted reading list — novelty screening (2025–2026 only)

The following studies are comparators, NOT proof the candidate question is uncovered.

1. Leveraging Crowdsourced Data for Extreme Heat Monitoring (2025).
   https://doi.org/10.1002/joc.70050
   Compare extreme heat observation retention and QC false positives.
2. A Machine Learning Approach Using Autoencoders to Perform Quality Control
   on Meteorological Data (2026).
   https://doi.org/10.1017/eds.2026.10030
   Compare meteorological anomaly detection and cross-station limitations.
3. Comprehensive Compilation and Quality Assessment of Street-Level
   Urban Air Temperature Measurements (2026).
   https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-06804-4
   Compare context-aware QC and credible extreme-temperature measurements.
4. CONUS-AgWeather: Quality-controlled data products (2026).
   https://www.nature.com/articles/s41597-026-07819-7
   Compare before/after QC impacts on downstream scientific calculations.

**Still to audit before claiming novelty:** event-level impact of identical
QC flags under alternative post-flag policies, heatwave event matching,
fragmentation / disappearance, censoring and clipping. Search synonyms
(flag treatment, quality-control policy, data censoring, missing day,
heatwave indices, continuity bias, event splitting, observation rejection).

Literature window here is limited to 2025–2026 by project choice, but a final
paper may still need older foundational definitions and historical context.
